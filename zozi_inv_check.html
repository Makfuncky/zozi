7️⃣ Share Capital & Funding Architecture (Why ZOZI Is Built This Way)

ZOZI is not a small shop seeking partners — it is a multi-country, multi-year infrastructure project. A project of this size cannot run on informal ownership; it runs on a priced share-capital system, where every share has a known price at a known stage. This is deliberate, and it protects every person who enters early.

The Price Ladder (value creation you can see)

StagePrice per 1%Window
Foundation700 OMRPre-launch • capped at 8% of company
Seed2,000 OMRPost-launch, post-audit
Growth roundsPriced on audited performanceSeries A and beyond

Each step-up is proof that value was created. A foundation share bought at 700 OMR is priced at 2,000 OMR the moment the platform launches — the early backer's gain is built into the structure, not promised.

Why Bank Loans Are Part of the Plan

Equity is for risk. Debt is for growth. Equity sells ownership forever — it is correct only when the risk is highest (now). Once the first annual accounts are audited, growth capital should come from banks, because a loan costs interest while equity costs the company permanently.

The 30–40K OMR facility in January 2027 therefore dilutes no one — it funds team, marketing and expansion on proven cash flow, and builds the corporate credit history that unlocks larger facilities later.

What this means for you: your 700 OMR today buys 1% at the lowest price ZOZI will ever offer. The share-capital discipline is exactly what keeps the company fundable — and your stake valuable — at every later stage.
